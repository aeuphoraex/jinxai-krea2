## What we actually operate

Velvet is a live companion-and-media service inside Second Life, built and run by JINXAI:

- **A wallet in L$.** Clients top up once (one debit to the studio treasury) and buy
  pictures and videos from that balance. Orders are priced in USD and settled in L$ at
  250 L$/USD; a picture is $2.49, a video $5.99, a cheap private test frame $0.40.
- **A wearable HUD.** A client wears one object, touches it, picks a performer, orders,
  and the file appears on her page. The HUD is branded: it renames itself to the wearer,
  or to "<performer>'s Client" on a copy an escort hands out.
- **A render studio.** Every order is generated, not archived: one still, one clip, 8
  steps at 1024/2048, and a per-image adapter fitted to that exact face so her next
  frame is her again.
- **A roster of real performers.** Consenting adult Second Life performers submit their
  own avatar stills, get a locked HUD to hand to their clients, and keep a share of
  every sale.
- **A public gallery.** SFW cards, distilled characters, and a marketplace of adapters —
  the shop window and the entry point.

This is not a concept deck. Orders, top-ups, refunds and renders are wired end to end
today; the wallet, the HUD and the studio are the same system.

## Why a media and technology group is the right first sponsor

Every order produces a still and a clip. That is a content pipeline with a customer at
the end of it: a sponsor is not buying banners, it is buying the library and the
surface it appears on. Second Life's club-and-creator layer — residents who already
pay for avatar media, DJ nights and club photography — is small, durable and
unusually willing to spend per unit. That is exactly the audience where a media brand
can be *useful* rather than wallpaper.

the sponsor surface; adult work stays on the private rail, behind the high-risk
processor), and **no fake numbers** — we report counters we can produce on request,
never modelled reach.
